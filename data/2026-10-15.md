Abstract: Reticular chemistry allows metal-organic frameworks (MOFs) to be designed from their building units, yet their synthesis remains constrained by sparse experimental data, noisy crystallization outcomes, and the absence of benchmarks for comparing active-learning strategies. While data-driven approaches such as Bayesian optimization (BO) can improve synthesis success rates, selecting appropriate optimizers beforehand for mapping MOF crystallization landscapes remains largely empirical. Here we present ActMOF, an experiment-grounded benchmark for comparing search strategies in aluminum-carboxylate MOF synthesis. ActMOF is emulated from experimentally measured microwave syntheses with each encoded condition assigned a crystallinity index calculated based on diffraction peak intensity and full width at half-maximum. By benchmarking policies guided by Gaussian processes (GP), tree ensembles and large language models (LLM), ActMOF placed the GP family among the leading policies for active learning in sparse synthesis landscapes. It then demonstrated transferability by guiding the microwave synthesis of six reticularly related aluminum MOFs. All six campaigns gave crystalline products within a fixed budget of 12 experiments each, and established a reusable testbed for AI-driven MOF synthesis.

Abstract: Interpreting electron paramagnetic resonance (EPR) spectra commonly requires repeated forward simulation and optimization of spin Hamiltonian parameters. Established software provides mature simulation and fitting methods, but these calculations are not generally available as differentiable components within machine-learning workflows. We present TorchSpin, an open-source PyTorch framework that ports the organization and numerical conventions of EasySpin into a tensor-based computational environment. Representative calculations spanning continuous-wave EPR, ENDOR, pulse EPR, spin dynamics, trajectory simulation, and magnetometry closely reproduced corresponding EasySpin results. In matched benchmarks, TorchSpin CPU execution was faster than EasySpin for six of nine workloads although performance depended strongly on the calculation. We therefore focused on the capabilities enabled by automatic differentiation in PyTorch. In a Cu(II) powder-spectrum demonstration, feedback propagated through TorchSpin improved the quality of a supervised convolutional neural network (CNN) without using parameter labels during refinement. Models trained from randomly initialized CNNs using only noisy spectra and TorchSpin reconstruction loss achieved median similarities of $\geq$ 0.99. CNNs trained on TorchSpin-generated libraries also transferred without adaptation to independently generated EasySpin spectra with performance comparable to EasySpin-trained controls. Once trained, the CNN produced initial parameter estimates in approximately 1 ms per spectrum while a batch of 512 was processed in approximately 1.3 ms with GPU acceleration. TorchSpin therefore provides both a tested Python implementation of established EPR calculations and a differentiable physical layer for rapid learned inference, spectral refinement, and parameter-label-free training.

Abstract: Generating novel molecules with desired properties is a central challenge in drug discovery. Although foundational work focused on de novo generation, practical use cases require a more complex setting: conditioning on an initial seed molecule, optimizing multiple properties, and operating in data-sparse regimes. We introduce PairIT, a flexible training framework for autoregressive transformers over SMILES sequences that enables seeded generation by learning from pairs of structurally similar molecules. By leveraging matched pairs, the model learns to generate local, chemically meaningful edits that improve a property of the seed molecule. Prompt tokens extend this framework by specifying the target property, desired direction, and approximate magnitude of change. Multiple prompt tokens can be concatenated to enable multi-property optimization at inference time. For properties where pair supervision alone is insufficient, reinforcement learning post-training can further improve optimization, providing a tunable trade-off between proximity to the seed and the optimization objective. PairIT naturally supports pretraining on a large dataset and fine-tuning on a smaller dataset, which is critical for performance in data-sparse regimes. Surprisingly, we observe diminishing returns to the scale of the pretraining dataset. We demonstrate empirically on MoleculeNet and Therapeutics Data Commons datasets that, with appropriate paired-data curation, a standard transformer model of modest size (68M parameters) can achieve robust seeded molecular optimization in low-data regimes.
